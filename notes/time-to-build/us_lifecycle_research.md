# The Lifecycle of U.S. Data Center Projects: Stages, Data Sources, and Durations

## TL;DR
- A U.S. data center moves through ~10 stages (site/land → power procurement & interconnection → zoning/permitting/environmental → tax incentives → financing → design → long-lead equipment procurement → construction → commissioning → phased energization/IT install → ramp). Total time from plan to operation is typically 3–6 years; construction alone runs ~18–36 months, but the binding constraint has shifted decisively from construction to **power** (generation interconnection now takes a median 61 months from request to commercial operation per Berkeley Lab) and to **long-lead electrical equipment** (power transformers ~128 weeks, extra-high-voltage units up to 60 months).
- For measurement, the most important public sources are the Census Bureau's **Value of Construction Put in Place (C30)** — which since July 2024 breaks out data centers as a category and whose underlying **Construction Progress Reporting Survey (CPRS)** captures start dates, projected completion, total cost, and monthly value-in-place — and **BEA's NIPA fixed investment** (structures + computers/peripherals). The authoritative synthesis is the **Fed's FEDS 2025-109 paper**, which uses Dodge Construction Network project microdata to estimate ~11-month plan-to-start (15 for large projects), ~20-month start-to-completion (large), and a ~33% abandonment rate. Rich stage-date detail lives in proprietary trackers (Dodge, CBRE, JLL, datacenterHawk, DC Byte, Baxtel, Synergy, SemiAnalysis) and **restricted Census CPRS microdata via the FSRDC**.
- Salient AI campuses show a bimodal timeline: speed-optimized builds bypassing the grid with on-site gas (xAI Colossus: 122 days to first operation; Meta Prometheus; Stargate Abilene ~16 months land-to-operation per building) versus grid-dependent builds constrained to multi-year timelines. Spending is front-loaded on structures (Census: ~50% of a >$100M/3-yr project's value in year 1, ~90% by end of year 2) but the IT equipment (servers/GPUs — the majority of total cost) is back-loaded to just before energization.

## Key Findings

1. **The lifecycle is heavily parallel-pathed, and power is now the critical path.** Site selection increasingly follows power availability rather than the reverse. Developers routinely order transformers and switchgear at land close (6–12 months before groundbreaking) and start vertical construction while long-lead gear is still in fabrication.

2. **Two divergent timeline models have emerged.** Grid-dependent projects face median generation-interconnection waits exceeding five years (Berkeley Lab, "Queued Up 2026"). Speed-optimized AI campuses bypass this with on-site gas generation, compressing time-to-first-power to months — xAI's Colossus reached first operation 122 days after construction start.

3. **Census C30 + CPRS is the authoritative public spine for structures spending and phasing**, and BEA converts it into GDP investment. But neither captures the IT equipment (servers/GPUs) that is the majority of data center capex; that must be inferred (the Fed FEDS 2025-109 method).

4. **The best micro-level timing data (stage dates) is proprietary** — Dodge Construction Network (which also feeds Census), CBRE/JLL (MW under construction, preleasing, absorption), and Epoch AI (the leading *public* satellite-based tracker of the largest AI campuses).

5. **Durations are bifurcating**: interconnection and equipment lead times have lengthened dramatically since 2020, while physical shell/fit-out construction has, if anything, accelerated for speed-prioritizing builders. Per a JPMorgan analysis (data as of April 9, 2026), more than 60% of data-center capacity planned for completion in 2027 was not yet under construction, with another 7% delayed; per Sightline Climate data cited by Bloomberg (April 1, 2026), of ~12 GW slated to come online in the U.S. in 2026, only about one-third (~5 GW across 140 tracked projects) was under active construction.

## Details

### PART 1 — THE STEPS AND THE PROCESS

**Stage 0 — Strategy / build model.** The first decision is build-to-suit vs. speculative ("spec"), and self-build (hyperscaler) vs. colocation/wholesale vs. large dedicated AI campus. This shapes everything downstream.

**Stage 1 — Site selection & land acquisition (≈3–6 months, power-gated).** Criteria: power availability, fiber, water for cooling, land for expansion, hazard risk, and incentives. In the AI era, developers increasingly buy "powered land" — sites with existing grid capacity or an energized substation — at a premium. Acquisition is often done quietly through LLCs before public announcement (in the xAI Memphis case, land was bought ~6 months before the June 2024 announcement).

**Stage 2 — Power procurement & utility interconnection (the binding constraint).** This runs in parallel with everything and is now the dominant bottleneck. There are two distinct queues: (a) the **generation interconnection queue** (power plants seeking to connect), and (b) **large-load interconnection** (the data center itself connecting as a load).
- **Generation queues** (LBNL "Queued Up: 2026 Edition," data through end-2025): over 2,060 GW of total generation and storage capacity were actively seeking connection to the grid at end-2025. The median duration from interconnection request (IR) to commercial operation (COD) was over five years for projects built in 2025 — the 2025 median project took 61 months, versus 36 months in 2015 and 22 months in 2008. Success rates are low: only 13% of capacity that submitted interconnection requests from 2000–2019 had reached commercial operations by end-2024, while 77% had been withdrawn.
- **Large-load queues**: ERCOT's large-load interconnection requests rocketed to over 230 GW in 2025 (~4× the ~63 GW at end-2024), roughly 70–90% data centers; CenterPoint reported a 700% jump (1→8 GW) in about a year. PJM data centers drive ~94% of projected load growth through 2030; Northern Virginia large-load waits approach ~7 years.
- **Regulatory responses (2025–26)**: Texas SB 6 (signed June 20, 2025) overhauled large-load interconnection (screening-study fees ≥$100,000, curtailment obligations, ownership/financial disclosure); ERCOT moved to a "Batch Study"/Batch Zero process (target effective Aug 1, 2026). FERC found PJM's co-located-load tariff unjust and unreasonable (Docket EL25-49, final order Dec 18, 2025, 193 FERC ¶ 61,217); PJM proposed an Expedited Interconnection Track (filed Feb 27, 2026) targeting ~10-month Generator Interconnection Agreements for up to 10 large-load-tied generation requests per year. FERC Order 2023 (issued July 28, 2023, 184 FERC ¶ 61,054) imposed first-ready-first-served cluster studies and firm 150-day study deadlines with financial penalties for late studies. DOE directed FERC to open a national large-load interconnection rulemaking (Docket RM26-4, Oct 2025). Texas Gov. Abbott paused new data center grid connections in August 2026 pending a PUCT/ERCOT audit.
- **Bypass strategies**: on-site/behind-the-meter gas generation and battery storage (BTM) have reportedly cut time-to-energization from 5–7 years to 18–24 months for some projects.

**Stage 3 — Zoning, permitting, environmental review, local approvals (≈6–18 months).** Handled mostly at county/municipal level; conditional-use permits, rezoning, variances (building height, noise), and environmental review (water, air, emissions). Rising community opposition and moratoria (e.g., Boone County, IN) are lengthening this stage. Example: Edged Energy's Polk County, IA facility was proposed April 2024, approved March 2025, targeting June 2027 completion.

**Stage 4 — Tax incentive negotiation (parallel, months to >1 year).** Per KPMG, at least 38 states offer data center incentive programs — typically sales/use tax exemptions on equipment, property tax abatement, and utility exemptions. Negotiation is timed to align with the "go-live" date; state economic development agencies lead. Virginia's Data Center Retail Sales & Use Tax exemption requires an MOU with VEDP before equipment purchases qualify. In 2026, backlash produced pauses/restrictions in Ohio, Illinois (effective July 1, 2026), and Massachusetts.

**Stage 5 — Financing.** Hyperscale self-build is funded from corporate capex; large AI campuses increasingly use JVs and private credit (Meta–Blue Owl JV for Hyperion; Blackstone/QTS; SoftBank/MGX/Oracle for Stargate).

**Stage 6 — Design & engineering (≈6–18 months, overlaps procurement).** Schematic → detailed construction documents → commissioning plan. The core design step is ~4–10 weeks per DataX Connect, but full design/development spans 9–18 months.

**Stage 7 — Long-lead equipment procurement (now a critical-path gate).** Must be ordered before groundbreaking. Per Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey, standard power transformers average 128 weeks for delivery and generator step-up (GSU) transformers average 144 weeks, with extra-high-voltage units up to 60 months; Wood Mackenzie modeled a 30% supply deficit for U.S. power transformers in 2025. By Q1 2026 GSU lead times had pushed past 160 weeks; high-voltage circuit breakers reached 125 weeks in H2 2025 (versus 77 weeks in 2023). Medium-voltage data-center-class switchgear runs 52–104 weeks (versus ~24 weeks pre-COVID). Consequences: only about a third of ~12 GW of 2026 capacity was under active construction (Sightline/Bloomberg), and Sightline estimates 30–50% of planned 2026 openings will slip or cancel.

**Stage 8 — Construction (shell then MEP fit-out; ≈12–36 months).** Sequence: sitework/foundations → shell/envelope (dried-in) → electrical/mechanical rough-in and fit-out → integrated systems testing. A single building runs ~12–24 months; multi-building campuses extend toward ~36 months. The back half carries most labor (power distribution, UPS, generators, cooling, CRAHs).

**Stage 9 — Commissioning (≈3–6 months).** Levels 1–5 testing; load-bank tests of generators/UPS; integrated systems test; owner's letter of acceptance.

**Stage 10 — Phased energization & IT (server/GPU) install.** Large campuses turn over in phases; power is energized building-by-building and racks installed just before/at go-live. This is where the bulk of dollar value (GPUs) lands, and it is back-loaded.

**Stage 11 — Ramp to full operation.** For colocation, operation "begins in earnest once customers occupy the space." For AI campuses, ramp is governed by chip-delivery cadence and power availability, often stretching build-out over years (Meta Hyperion to ~2030–2032).

**Segment differences:**
- *Hyperscale self-build*: largest, longest, most vertically integrated; increasingly self-powered.
- *Colocation/wholesale*: ~18–30 months; now ~73–80% preleased before completion (CBRE H1 2026: 80.4% of primary-market under-construction capacity preleased; JLL: 73%), so "build-it-and-they-will-come" is dead.
- *Large AI campuses*: gigawatt-scale, multi-year, either grid-bypassing (speed) or grid-dependent (slower).

### PART 2 — DATA SOURCES

**A. Census Bureau — Value of Construction Put in Place (C30 / VIP).** *Measures:* monthly dollar value of construction work done. *Data center category:* since July 1, 2024, data centers are broken out as a separate line within private office construction (in the "Historical Value Put in Place" tables). *Frequency:* monthly, released first business day two months after the reference month; SAAR and unadjusted; two prior months revised each release. *Coverage:* national only (no state/metro). *Access:* free/public. Data center structures spending rose from a $39.8B SAAR (April 2025) to a $50.7B SAAR (April 2026), overtaking general office ($43.8B). *Limitation:* structures only — excludes servers/GPUs; national-level only.

**B. Census — Construction Progress Reporting Survey (CPRS): the C30 companion the requester asked about.** This is the survey underlying C30's private nonresidential estimates. Owners of sampled projects (drawn from Dodge project lists) report **start date, projected completion date, total cost estimate, and monthly value of work done from start to completion** — precisely the "construction part" companion. *Access:* aggregate outputs public; **project-level microdata is confidential/restricted**, available only through the Federal Statistical Research Data Centers (FSRDC) with an approved project. **FLAG — restricted CPRS microdata is highly valuable and not publicly accessible.** It would allow direct measurement of data-center-specific start-to-completion durations and the monthly spending profile, isolating data centers from other nonresidential projects, which the public tables cannot do.
- Census also publishes a **public phasing table** (Table 4, "Percent Distribution of Value Put in Place Each Month from Start to Completion," by project value band). For the largest projects (≥$100M), the cumulative curve is: ~2.8% in the month of start, ~23.5% by month 6, ~48.9% by month 12, ~71.1% by month 18, ~86.5% by month 24, ~95.1% by month 30 — roughly linear-to-slightly-front-loaded spend over 3+ years. This is the single best *public* source for the structures spending profile.

**C. BEA — NIPA fixed investment.** *Measures:* data center structures investment (from the Census CPIP report) plus computers/peripheral equipment (not separately broken out for data centers). *Frequency:* quarterly (GDP release). *Access:* public (bea.gov, FRED). *Limitation:* no single "data center investment" line; equipment must be imputed.

**D. Federal Reserve FEDS 2025-109 (Brandsaas, Garcia, Kurtzman, Nichols & Zytek, "Estimating Aggregate Data Center Investment with Project-level Data," Dec 17, 2025) — the authoritative methodological source.** Uses Dodge project microdata to nowcast aggregate data center investment. Key parameters: plan-to-start ~11 months (all), ~15 months (large >$100M projects); start-to-completion ~20 months (large); abandonment rate ~33%. Nowcast: investment rising from ~$60B (2024Q4) to ~$180B (2025Q4) to ~$370B annualized by 2026Q2; 2027 scenarios span $360B–$930B depending on the assumed inflow of new plans. Also provides a method to impute data center equipment investment in the NIPAs by detrending the computers/peripherals series (2015–2022 trend). *Access:* public.

**E. Dodge Construction Network (proprietary).** *Measures:* project-level pipeline with **stage dates — planning, bidding, start (groundbreaking), completion** — plus location, value, owner, and contractors. The Dodge Momentum Index (DMI) leads construction spending by ~12 months. *Coverage:* 10M+ historical projects, 400–500 field specialists, projects back to 1891. *Access:* **subscription/proprietary** (Dodge One platform). **FLAG — Dodge is the key proprietary source:** it is both the Census's project-sampling frame (55+ years) and the input to the Fed's investment model, yet the granular stage-date microdata is paywalled. Valuable because it is the only comprehensive source of plan→start→completion dates across the whole market. Through H1 2024 Dodge counted 78 data center project starts (>$9B, ~12M sq ft), a record.

**F. Commercial real estate trackers (subscription).** CBRE and JLL "State of the Market"/Global Data Center Trends: MW under construction, preleasing %, net absorption, vacancy, asking rents, by metro. CBRE H2 2025: primary-market supply +36% YoY to 9,432 MW; record 2,497.6 MW net absorption; vacancy 1.4%; 80.4% of under-construction capacity preleased (H1 2026); ~7,481 MW under construction (H1 2026). JLL: 7.8 GW pipeline, 73% preleased. *Access:* headline figures public; detailed data by subscription.

**G. Specialized data center trackers.**
- *Epoch AI Frontier Data Centers Hub* — **public**, satellite + permit based, tracks the largest AI campuses with construction start/operation dates, IT power, compute (H100-equivalents), and capital-cost timelines. The best public micro-tracker for salient projects; updated through 2026. It notes the 13 largest tracked U.S. sites hold ~2.5M H100e (~15% of global stock).
- *datacenterHawk, DC Byte, Baxtel, Data Center Map, Synergy Research, Structure Research, 451 Research/S&P Global* — **subscription** facility-level inventory, pipeline, operational status, power capacity. Baxtel: 8,000+ facilities across 600 regions. DC Byte: committed vs. under-construction pipeline across 135+ countries (flagging that committed supply now far exceeds under-construction capacity). Synergy: >1,000 operational hyperscale facilities, +120–130/yr forecast.
- *SemiAnalysis Data Center Model* — **proprietary/paid**, satellite-based, sold to companies. **FLAG — valuable, not public.**
- *ConstructConnect, Industrial Info Resources (IIR)* — **subscription** project databases focused on the active bidding/construction stages (ConstructConnect skews later-stage than Dodge).

**H. Utility & ISO/RTO filings (public).** ERCOT large-load queue and Batch reports, PJM planning/queue data, LBNL "Queued Up," FERC dockets (EL25-49, RM26-4), and state PUC filings. Third-party aggregators (interconnection.fyi, DC Hub) compile these. Utility integrated resource plans and interconnection agreements reveal project-specific power timelines. *Access:* public but fragmented and often codenamed.

**I. Company filings (public).** 10-Ks/10-Qs and earnings calls give capex guidance: 2026 capex — Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175–205B, Microsoft ~$175–190B, Meta $115–145B; the four hyperscalers ~$725B combined (up ~77% YoY from ~$410B in 2025). *Limitation:* capex is not broken out by facility or by structures vs. equipment; Microsoft's July 2026 extension of asset useful lives (15→25 yrs) plus lease reclassification lowered *reported* capex (~$190B → ~$175B) without changing underlying investment.

**J. EIA / DOE (public).** The DOE/LBNL "2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report" (December 2024, lead researcher Arman Shehabi) is the headline energy source: U.S. data center energy use reached 176 TWh by 2023, representing 4.4% of total U.S. electricity consumption, and is projected to roughly 325–580 TWh by 2028 (6.7%–12.0% of U.S. electricity). EIA's January 2026 STEO release forecast the strongest four-year U.S. electricity-demand growth since 2000, "fueled by data centers"; AEO2026 (April 8, 2026) estimated data center servers alone at ~7% of commercial-sector electricity in 2025.

**K. Satellite imagery-based tracking (mixed access).** Epoch AI (public), SemiAnalysis (paid), and academic/FAS work identify land clearing, roofing, cooling infrastructure (chillers, cooling towers), backup generators, and substations to date construction stages independently of company disclosure. Epoch estimates capacity from cooling-equipment dimensions with acknowledged error bands.

### PART 3 — DURATION ESTIMATES

**General / industry ranges (and how they've changed):**
- Full lifecycle (plan → operation): 3–6 years.
- Construction: 18–30 months (mid-size); 24–36 months (hyperscale); single building 12–24 months; large multi-building campuses toward 36 months.
- Component steps: site selection 3–6 months; design 6–18 months; permitting 6–18 months; commissioning 3–6 months.
- Fed FEDS 2025-109 (Dodge data): plan-to-start ~11 months (all) / ~15 (large); start-to-completion ~20 months (large); ~33% abandonment.
- **Lengthening:** generation-interconnection median 22 months (2008) → 36 months (2015) → 61 months (2025 projects) per LBNL; large-load waits 3–7 yrs; power transformers ~1 yr (2020) → 128 weeks, GSUs 144→>160 weeks, HV circuit breakers 77→125 weeks, switchgear ~24→52–104 weeks. JLL: more than half of 2025 projects had ≥3-month construction delays; grid-connection lead times exceed four years in primary markets.
- **Accelerating:** speed-optimized shell/fit-out; e-shelter built one Frankfurt building in 9 months; xAI's self-powered model compresses to weeks/months.
- **Reality check:** JPMorgan (April 2026) — more than 60% of capacity planned for 2027 completion not yet under construction, another 7% delayed; Sightline/Bloomberg — only ~1/3 of ~12 GW slated for 2026 under active construction; Goldman Sachs — only ~half of AI capacity targeted for 2025–2028 expected on time.

**Salient project timelines:**

*xAI Colossus (Memphis, TN):* Land bought Dec 2023 (~$35M, via a quiet LLC); Musk finalized Memphis ~March 2024; construction began ~March/April 2024; public announcement June 5, 2024; MLGW upgraded service to 50 MW then committed 150 MW (July 9, 2024); Phase 1 substation built in 97 days (complete Aug 2024, $24M); **Colossus 1 online with 100,000 H100 GPUs 122 days after construction start**, then doubled to 200,000 GPUs in 92 more days. Musk had rejected initial 18–24-month quotes. Relies on on-site gas turbines (bypassing the grid). Colossus 2 (Southaven, MS) scaling toward ~550,000 GPUs / ~2 GW.

*Stargate Abilene (OpenAI/Oracle/Crusoe/Lancium, TX):* Announced Jan 2025 (White House); Crusoe expansion plan March 18, 2025 (8 buildings, ~4M sq ft, ~1.2 GW); first two buildings energized/operational Sept 2025 (~200 MW, first GB200 racks). Epoch AI estimates ~16 months (1 yr 4 mo) from land clearing to operational per building; ~0.3 GW operational late 2025, targeting 0.6 GW ~May 2026 and full 1.2 GW Q4 2026. Estimated cost $15.9B (421 MW IT) rising to $31.9B (843 MW).

*Meta Prometheus (New Albany, OH):* ~1 GW AI supercluster; on-site behind-the-meter natural gas (partnered with Williams), a "tents-over-buildings" speed-to-market design; targeted online 2026.

*Meta Hyperion (Richland Parish, LA):* Land acquired 2024; construction started end-2024; announced June/July 2025; expanded July 13, 2026 to 5 GW / >$50B (from 2 GW / $27B), with a Blue Owl JV. Targets: 1.5 GW IT power by end-2027; 2 GW by 2030; full 5 GW ~2032. Entergy is building up to 10 gas plants. Construction runs through ~2030.

*Microsoft Fairwater (Mount Pleasant, WI):* Announced 2024 ($3.3B Phase 1); increased to $7.3B (Sept 2025); Nadella said the site went live "ahead of schedule" (April 16, 2026). Epoch AI: ~446k H100e / 369 MW IT at ~$14.0B initially, projected 2,263 MW / $85.7B by Q2 2028; 315 acres, three buildings, 1.2M sq ft. A second Wisconsin campus ($4B) was announced Sept 2025.

*Amazon Project Rainier (New Carlisle, IN):* Construction began Sept 2024; within ~7 months, 7 of 30 planned buildings were complete/operational; operational Oct 2025; $11B; 1,200 acres; 2.2 GW; ~500k Trainium2 chips for Anthropic; high-voltage delivered by Indiana Michigan Power (AEP).

*Google:* Botetourt County, VA — announced 2024/2025; first grading permit applications Feb 2026, land-disturbance permit issued Aug 2026, grading Fall 2026, first building completion expected ~2028 (multi-phase). Google also has Kansas City, Fort Wayne, Michigan City, and Morgan County projects; DCD cites a typical Google project at 18–24 months. Google's methodical, grid-connected model contrasts sharply with the AI-campus speed model.

**Spending profile over construction:** Census Table 4 (structures only) shows ~50% of a large (>$100M, >3-yr) project's value phased in by end of year 1 and ~90% by end of year 2 — moderately front-loaded. The Fed paper assumes even monthly spend for tractability but explicitly notes equipment is more back-loaded than structures; because IT equipment (GPUs) is the majority of total cost and is installed just before energization, *total* data-center capex is more back-loaded and lumpier than the structures curve implies. Census measures structures on a value-in-place (progress) basis, but equipment enters NIPAs at time of purchase — an important timing distinction for anyone reconciling the two.

## Recommendations

**For a central bank macroeconomist tracking AI/data center investment, a staged approach:**

1. **Anchor on the public official spine now.** Use the Census C30 data-center structures series (monthly) plus BEA NIPA structures, and adopt the Fed FEDS 2025-109 imputation to add equipment. Track the Census Table 4 phasing curve for the structures spending profile. This yields a defensible, GDP-consistent baseline that is available today at no cost.

2. **Replicate the Fed nowcasting method.** Its three parameters (plan-to-start ~11–15 mo, start-to-completion ~20 mo, abandonment ~33%) applied to the project pipeline are the best real-time turning-point estimator. If budget allows, license Dodge microdata (the same input) to run it in-house and to isolate data-center stage dates. *Benchmark that would change the call:* if the abandonment rate moves materially above ~33%, or plan-to-start lengthens beyond ~15 months, revise nowcasts downward.

3. **Apply for FSRDC access to restricted CPRS microdata.** This is the only way to directly measure data-center-specific start-to-completion durations and the monthly value-in-place, cleanly isolating data centers from other nonresidential structures. It is the highest-value non-public dataset for this exact research question.

4. **Layer in Epoch AI (free) for the largest AI campuses** to get satellite-verified start / first-power / operational dates independent of company PR, and CBRE/JLL for MW pipeline, preleasing, and absorption. Use utility/ISO filings (ERCOT, PJM, LBNL "Queued Up," FERC dockets EL25-49 and RM26-4) to monitor the power bottleneck directly.

5. **Monitor the leading indicators for inflection.** Dodge Momentum Index (leads spending ~12 months); hyperscaler capex guidance (leads equipment); interconnection queue times and transformer/switchgear lead times (lengthening = future delays); preleasing % (demand strength). Thresholds that would shift the outlook: a sustained DMI downturn, capex guidance cuts, a jump in abandonment/cancellation rates, or an easing of transformer lead times back below ~1 year (signaling supply catch-up).

## Caveats
- **No single source captures total data center investment.** Structures are measured; IT equipment (the majority) is imputed. All equipment figures involve modeling assumptions, and the Fed's imputation could over- or under-count if non-data-center computer purchases move off trend.
- **Company-reported project figures are announcements, not audited measurements.** Headline GW and dollar figures (e.g., Hyperion "5 GW / $50B") describe intended scale and often change or slip; treat them as planning envelopes, not energized capacity.
- **Satellite/tracker capacity estimates are inferred** from cooling equipment and permits, with acknowledged error bands; per-site IT load is not disclosed by hyperscalers, so campus rankings are best-effort reconstructions.
- **Forward figures are projections, not realized outcomes** (Fed 2027 range $360–930B; DOE 2028 electricity share 6.7–12%; JLL/JPMorgan/Goldman/Sightline delay estimates). The wide ranges reflect genuine uncertainty about future plan inflows and grid/equipment constraints.
- **Interconnection "queue" totals conflate generation and load** and include speculative projects; historically only ~13% of queued generation (2000–2019 cohort) ever connected and ~77% was withdrawn, so raw GW totals badly overstate the real pipeline.
- **Duration ranges are heterogeneous.** The bifurcation between grid-bypassing speed builds and grid-dependent builds means simple "averages" can mislead. Data vintages here span December 2024 through September 2026; the fastest-moving figures (queues, equipment lead times, capex) change quarterly and should be refreshed at each data release.
