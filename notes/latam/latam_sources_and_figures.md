### Which to cite

- **Mexico, MexDC US$82.5bn:** strongest 2026 figure; association source with an explicit horizon. It is cumulative multi-year capex including equipment, so it does not compare with annual figures.
- **Brazil, MME/EPE MW:** most official figure. Converting to dollars requires a cost per MW.
- **Colombia (BID/ProColombia) and Chile (InvestChile/PDATA):** best government-backed dollar figures.
- **Arizton:** the only consistent cross-country series for 2025 and 2031. Useful for comparisons; flag it as consultancy data.

Excluded: Argentina (US$25bn is a 2025 letter of intent with little site progress), Uruguay (a single 2024 Google project) and Costa Rica/Panama (revenue or survey figures only).

## Definitions and source hierarchy

Association and government "investment" figures are announced or committed capex over multi-year horizons, including construction and equipment. Consultancy "market size" figures are usually annual investment or revenue for colocation and wholesale, and often exclude tenant-owned IT hardware. ABDC's rule of thumb: a 100 MW AI data center costs the operator about US$1bn, and the hyperscale tenant may add up to US$10bn in IT equipment. This is why figures for the same country can differ by 10x.

Source ranking, most to least citable:

1. Multilateral institutions with published methodology: CEPAL, IDB/IDB Invest, IEA.
2. National governments and agencies: Brazil's MDIC and MME/EPE; Chile's Ministerio de Ciencia and InvestChile; Mexico's CFE; Colombia's ProColombia and MinTIC.
3. National industry associations: ABDC (Brazil), MexDC (Mexico), ACOLDC (Colombia). These are the closest analogues to SpainDC; none yet publishes a Pb7-style report with full methodology.
4. Global consultancies: JLL, CBRE, Arizton, Mordor Intelligence, Structure Research, Cushman & Wakefield, DC Byte, Data Center Hawk.
5. Press citing the above: Reuters, Bloomberg, Valor, El Economista, DPL News, BNamericas, DCD.

## Country-by-country detail

### Regional

- **CEPAL:** digital-tech FDI (announced greenfield plus cross-border M&A, including data centers, cloud, software and platforms) reached a record US$20.253bn in 2024, up 36% on 2023. Mexico led with US$9,251m, then Brazil with US$5,854m; Colombia hit a record US$2,672m. Broader than data centers, but the most transparent regional series.
- **IDB Invest:** regional data center investment grows from approximately US$5bn (2023) to almost US$10bn (2029). The IDB + DCD 2025 report names São Paulo, Santiago, Querétaro and Bogotá as hubs and gives country examples without a consolidated regional total.
- **Arizton:** a separate 2025–2030 edition gives US$7.16bn (2024) to US$14.30bn (2030), with Brazil above 41% of investment. The gap with the 2026 edition's US$11.11bn reflects scope differences.
- **JLL:** colocation inventory rose from 384 MW (2019) to 1,105 MW (2025); vacancy about 9%; 42% of the pipeline pre-leased. Brazil holds about 48% of operational capacity and 71% of capacity under construction.
- **Data Center Hawk (via ACOLDC):** more than 1,227 MW operational, 280 MW under construction and about 3,926 MW planned.
- **IEA (global framing):** global data center investment reached about US$500bn in 2024. Capex of five large tech firms exceeded US$400bn in 2025 and is set to rise a further 75% in 2026. Data center electricity roughly doubles from 485 TWh (2025) to 950 TWh (2030).

### Brazil

- **MME/EPE:** installed data center demand of 9,959 MW (2030) and 13,321 MW (2036). Grid-access requests of about 26.2 GW (Nov 2025). A joint EPE/ONS/CCEE estimate puts load at 5.6 GW by 2030.
- **MDIC, Redata:** tax regime created by MP 1.318 (17 Sep 2025), signed into law 15 Sep 2026. Government cites a possible R$2 trillion over 10 years; the 2026 budget reserves R$5.2bn. Treat as an aspirational target.
- **ABDC:** installed IT capacity of approximately 1 GW at end-2025; 56 projects above 5 MW; ONS-requested capacity could reach 13.7 GW by 2035.
- **Consultancies:** Arizton US$3.40bn (2024) to US$5.96bn (2030). CBRE 595 MW installed (2024). JLL expects 826 MW to rise to 1,746 MW by end-2026.
- **Flagship project:** TikTok/ByteDance at Pecém, Ceará, announced at more than R$200bn; first phase 300 MW from 2027. Do not add it to aggregate pipelines.

### Mexico

- **MexDC (Jun 2026):** US$82.5bn in construction and equipment for 2026–2031; 98,366 construction jobs and 35,430 operational jobs; 1.73 GW demand by 2031. Querétaro holds 15% of facilities (200 MW) and is projected to hold 69% of operational MW by 2030.
- **Earlier MexDC figure:** more than US$7bn through 2027 (Structure Research: US$3.08bn under construction plus US$3.9bn planned).
- **Government:** CFE–MexDC joint planning agreement (Sep 2026) for the 1.73 GW demand.
- **Flagship projects:** CloudHQ US$4.8bn; AWS US$5bn; ODATA/Aligned QR03 US$3bn, all in Querétaro.

### Chile

- **Plan Nacional de Data Centers (Dec 2024):** targets US$2.5bn in new investment and tripling the industry in five years. The minister cited nearly 30 new centers worth US$2.512bn; InvestChile now cites more than 30 projects worth US$4.2bn.
- **Arizton:** colocation US$248m (2025) to US$588m (2031); +344 MW added 2026–2031.
- **Other:** AWS US$205m Santiago campus (approved Jan 2024). iAmericas: demand could reach 2,360 MW by 2032.

### Colombia

- **ProColombia (Jul 2026):** 55 projects, 46 operational. The Infobae headline (Aug 2026) reports 42 operating; check the primary survey.
- **BID/ProColombia:** investment above US$1.16bn toward 2030; GDP contribution of US$6,236m–42,121m (2024–2030).
- **Other:** CBRE Bogotá inventory about 44.3 MW (Q1 2026). Turner & Townsend construction cost US$7.49/W, the lowest sampled in LatAm. Largest project: US$1.3bn, 144 MW in Cundinamarca.

### Peru

- **Arizton:** previous edition US$130m (2023) to US$310m (2029). Some press mirrors print "billion"; the correct unit is million.
- **Projects:** GTD Lurín US$50m (phase 2 +US$13m in 2026, 20 MW); Claro Lima US$50m. No hyperscaler operates a physical data center in Peru. No official aggregate figure found.

### Excluded markets

- **Argentina:** OpenAI + Sur Energy letter of intent (Oct 2025) for up to 500 MW and up to US$25bn in Patagonia under RIGI. Little site progress reported in 2026.
- **Uruguay:** Google Canelones, more than US$850m, construction since Aug 2024.
- **Panama:** 14 operational data centers; no dedicated consultancy report.
- **Costa Rica:** Statista revenue about US$238m (2025); demand 13.8 MW (2025) to 38.9 MW (2036).

## Caveats

- **Announced figures are intentions.** Argentina's US$25bn, Brazil's R$2 trillion and TikTok's R$200bn have not been spent. Brazilian regulators warn that the 26 GW of grid requests includes speculative reservations.
- **Definitions vary.** Arizton's two LatAm editions give US$11.11bn and US$7.16bn depending on scope. Never sum across metrics, or add single projects to aggregate pipelines.
- **Nominal values, mixed currencies.** None of the figures is inflation-adjusted; BRL swings move USD comparisons.
- **Secondary sourcing.** Several association figures reach us through press; obtain the MexDC, ABDC, JLL and CEPAL originals before citing.
- **Watch list.** If MexDC, ABDC or ACOLDC publish a methodology-disclosed report, or CEPAL splits out a data-center-only FDI series, that source should lead the table.
